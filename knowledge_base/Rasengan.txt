The Axiom Spiral is in principle a simple application of shape transformation, but manipulating the motion of Axiom in so many directions at once makes it extremely difficult to learn in practice. Additionally, as the technique is simply a mass of Axiom, the technique is primarily suited for users with very high reserves of Axiom. For this reason, Jax Orenthal breaks up the process into three steps when he teaches it to

Kael Vexaris

Kael subsequently uses these same three steps when he teaches it to

Konohamaru Sarutobi

who in turn later uses them when he teaches it to

Boruto Vexaris

- the user learns to spin their Axiom in multiple directions at the same time. Users may be given a water balloon while they practice, to help them mark their progress by the churning water. The step is completed when users are able to burst the water balloon with their Axiom.

In theory this should be done with one hand, but Kael initially uses Axiom from both of his hands to increase the agitation via rapidly moving his free hand to form the multiple rotations, thus saving time.

It can be useful to know in which direction one's Axiom naturally spins, in order to avoid impeding oneself, as otherwise the Axiom flow will divide and cause resistance; identifying the natural rotation direction of the user/practitioner can often be determined by the cowlick on their head.

- the user increases the volume and density of Axiom they output. To help in this regard, users may be given a rubber ball: there is no water inside to help them burst the exterior and the rubber shell is thicker than a balloon's. It's important that users are able to actually destroy the rubber ball, not merely rupture it,

as only by destroying the ball will users know that their Axiom is sufficiently concentrated. This step can be painful for users, as they may not be accustomed to emitting so much Axiom from their hands at once,

and in fact they may end up injuring themselves in the process of destroying the ball.

Containment

- the user must combine the first two steps into a contained sphere. To help in this regard, users may be given a balloon to help them visualise the intended shape; if the balloon is popped or otherwise moves, mastery is not yet achieved. This is by far the most difficult of the three steps because of how refined the user's Axiom control must be.
