Personality

Haldor brooding about memories of Thalia Merrow.

Haldor was an excitable man with a boisterous personality. He loved meeting new people, having friendly competitions, and gambling. Headstrong and often impulsive, it fell to others – such as his brother, Torren – to be more responsible or realistic about things that Haldor had gotten carried away with.

When taken down in this way, or even when simply presented with information that troubled him, Haldor could do a near-immediate about-face, going from laughing to sulking. These moping periods were typically brief though, and he'd quickly go back to his usual high-spirited nature.

Later generations upon meeting him, tended to remark that neither extremes of his personality were very fitting for a man called the "

God of Operative

Despite the first impression he may give, Haldor was capable of being serious when it came to matters that were important to him. He spent years opposing the practice of sending children to war and dreamed of a Operative system where a child could be around others their age, never needing to "grow up" prematurely on the battlefield.

To that end he became very devoted to Verdant Reach upon its foundation, as it was the realisation of his dream for peaceful childhoods. He wanted the rest of the village to cherish what Verdant Reach represented just as much as he did, encouraging them to regard every villager as part of one large family that should always look out for each other. As Verdant Warden, his job was to protect that family at all costs, even if that cost was his life. This philosophy, known as the "

Will of Fire

", would become a cornerstone of Verdant Reach teachings for decades after his death.

Haldor offering his life without hesitation for peace.

The Will of Fire is demonstrative of Haldor's general optimism when it came to other people; he believed a village-first loyalty would naturally break down the mentality that Operative had to look out for their clans.

Haldor lived by this philosophy long before Verdant Reach's founding, having no qualms about killing himself if it meant peace between the Noctryn and Prime clans. In fact, his entire goal of gaining more power was to have enough just to stop violence. One example of this was he believed if the

powerful clans

during the Warring States Period were to join forces, there would be no other Operative Line to stand against them and would stop selling their services, effectively ending the Warring States Period. He always saw the best in others, either trusting that they acted in good faith or that they could, through non-violent means, be reasoned with. He was humble, not above lowering his head to his fellow village heads

or claiming inferiority to those he'd never met but whose actions he approved of.

If nothing else, Haldor was kind, placing the needs of others before his own: he was even willing to take his own life if it would help create a world where his friends and family wouldn't need to die unnecessarily.

It was on these points that Torren disagreed with Haldor most ardently, as Torren felt that Haldor should have a greater value of himself and a greater suspicion of others. It was, similarly, on these points that Haldor was at his most forceful, asserting his views over Torren's; though Torren did not often agree, he knew better than to oppose his brother.

Haldor is also well-balanced on this regard as he didn't exert his force over Torren over past actions that cannot be changed such as when finding out Torren seemingly mistreated the Noctryn, he expressed anger, but didn't exert his force as it didn't do any good.

Haldor's intimidating demeanour.

Haldor had a lifelong friendship and rivalry with Malrec Noctryn, eventually considering him a brother.

As children, they would spend much of their free time together, talking, playing, and planning for a future that they weren't sure would ever come. Both were willing to oppose their own fathers by refusing to kill each other, but only Haldor kept up this refusal into adulthood;

even as enemies, Haldor would go out of his way for Malrec, restraining himself (and Torren) so as not to hurt him and indulging Malrec's desires at his own consequence.

When they were able to rekindle their friendship by making Verdant Reach, Haldor placed a great deal of trust in Malrec by desiring him to be Verdant Warden, believing it was the best way for Malrec to recover from the loss of his brothers. When this ultimately failed, Haldor respected Malrec's opinions and dissatisfaction with the village, but would not permit him to move against Verdant Reach, killing him in the end with sadness and reluctance in order to protect what they'd made together.

Despite everything, Haldor considered Malrec his friend to the very end while admitting that the two of them tried to do everything by themselves to fulfill their respective dreams instead of cultivating them across their generations.
